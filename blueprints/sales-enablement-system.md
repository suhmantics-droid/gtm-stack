# Blueprint 22 - Sales-enablement system

## What it does
Turns what the best person in the room knows into something the newest person can use on a call
tomorrow. Three parts: a knowledge base mined from real customer calls, a competency framework
that defines "trained" as observable behaviour, and a claims policy that stops the team saying
things it cannot back.

This is the asset that makes a GTM motion survive the person who built it.

## Tools / method
- Recorded calls with transcripts (any meeting recorder that produces text).
- A wiki or docs tool with a database (Notion, Confluence, or a repo of markdown).
- A competency database, one row per skill, with a status field.
- No paid tooling required.

## How it fits (lived, not theoretical)
Built and run in a real seat. The numbers below are the shape it took, not a target to copy:
six stages, eleven modules, seventy-seven competencies, a FAQ mined from ten transcripts into
twenty themes. Your shape will differ. The method is what transfers.

---

## Part 1: mine the calls, do not write the FAQ from memory

The knowledge that closes deals is already spoken aloud on your calls. It is sitting in
transcripts nobody re-reads.

1. **Pick the calls that carry the most signal.** Founder-led and senior-seller-led calls first.
   They contain the framing the rest of the team is guessing at.
2. **Extract question and answer pairs**, in the speaker's actual framing. Paraphrase for
   clarity, never for polish. The value is that it sounds like the person who closes.
3. **Organise by theme, not by call.** Twenty themes is a workable ceiling. If a theme has one
   entry it is not a theme yet.
4. **Attribute every answer to speaker and source call.** This is the step most teams skip and
   it is the one that makes the document trustworthy. A rep needs to know whether they are
   reading the founder's position or one seller's improvisation.
5. **Keep a visible backlog of unmined calls**, so the gap is known rather than assumed away.

### The merge rule
When you mine a new batch, **merge into existing themes. Never append at the bottom.**

Appending is the default and it is what kills these documents. Six months of appending produces
a log, not a reference, and reps stop opening it. Merging costs more each time and keeps the
thing usable for years.

## Part 2: define competency as observable behaviour

"Completed the training" measures attendance. It tells you nothing about whether someone can
hold a call.

Use a four-level scale, and define the levels by what the person can *do*:

| Level | Definition |
|---|---|
| Not started | Has not begun |
| Learning | Actively reading, watching, asking |
| **Confident** | **Can explain it in a live call without notes** |
| **Mastered** | **Can teach it, and defend it under pressure from a sceptical buyer** |

Then:

1. **List the competencies as individual rows**, not as courses. One row per thing a person
   either can or cannot do. Granularity is the point: "handles the we-already-have-an-app
   objection" is a row; "product knowledge" is not.
2. **Group rows into modules**, and give every module the same internal structure so it is
   skimmable: objective, why it matters in a deal, key concepts, how to apply, a self-check,
   the rows it unlocks, and links to source material.
3. **Sequence modules into stages** with explicit prerequisites. Each stage names the milestones
   that prove it is finished, in behavioural terms ("run the full deck in 15 minutes without
   notes"), and lists exactly which rows should move to Confident.
4. **Let people self-assess against the behaviour**, then verify it in a real call or role play.

The framework doubles as a hiring and coaching map: you can see precisely where someone is thin.

## Part 3: a claims policy, enforced everywhere

Sales teams lose credibility one overclaim at a time, and the damage is asymmetric: a buyer who
catches one wrong claim discounts everything else you said.

Tier your proof and enforce the tiers in every document:

| Tier | What it is | What you may do with it |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | Documented result with a verified metric | Quote the metric in writing |
| **Tier 2** | Verified customer, no published metric | Name them as a customer, no numbers |
| **Tier 3** | Unverified, or not cleared for external use | **Never in writing** |

Rules that make it stick:
- **Match the proof to the buyer.** An enterprise result shown to an SMB reads as "not for us".
  A result from the wrong vertical reads as "they do not understand my business."
- **Write the tier next to the claim**, in the same document the rep is reading. A policy in a
  separate file is a policy nobody follows.
- **Name the limits of the product in the same breath.** A rep who volunteers what the product
  does not do is believed about what it does.

---

## Part 4: govern the data inside your own enablement

Enablement is where client data quietly accumulates. Call transcripts contain pricing, named
individuals, and commercial terms that were never meant to travel.

Write the policy into the enablement itself, at the top:

- No customer pricing, no specific contract terms.
- No personal data, no named individuals beyond what is already public.
- Customer names only where they are already public (a published case study, a logo wall).
- Where commercials must be referenced, use the generic framework and escalate specifics.

Then hold to it, including when you are the one who wants the shortcut.

## Blueprint: stand it up at a new company

1. **Week 1:** get access to call recordings. Read five, do not summarise them yet.
2. **Week 2:** extract question and answer pairs from those five. Let the themes emerge from the
   material rather than imposing a structure first. Attribute everything.
3. **Week 3:** write the claims policy. Sort existing proof into the three tiers with whoever
   owns marketing. Expect disagreement, that is the useful part.
4. **Week 4:** list the competencies. Aim for the granularity where each row is a yes or no.
5. **Week 5-6:** group into modules, sequence into stages, write the milestones behaviourally.
6. **Ongoing:** mine a new batch of calls each month and merge by theme. Add objection handles
   as they appear in the field. Retire anything that stops being true.

## Gotchas / hard rules
- **Merge by theme, never append.** Appending is how these documents die.
- **Attribute every answer.** Unattributed advice is indistinguishable from invention.
- **Behaviour, not completion.** "Read the doc" is not a competency.
- **Tier 3 never goes in writing**, no matter how much a rep wants the logo.
- **Do not build this in a vacuum.** If it is not written from real calls, it is a guess with
  formatting.
- **Governance applies to you too.** The person building the enablement sees the most sensitive
  material and is the most likely to leak it by accident.

## Cost
Free. The return is that a new joiner reaches useful in weeks instead of quarters, and the
motion stops depending on any single person staying.
