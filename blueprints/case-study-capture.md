# Blueprint 21 - Case-study capture

## What it does
Turns a win into a reference asset - the proof that lands the next engagement or role. A repeatable
way to document results from witnessed, verified material (not embellishment).

## Tools / method
- The measurement scorecard (Blueprint 17) - the verified numbers behind the story.
- A customer-journalism method (the operator-portrait approach) - build the narrative only from
  witnessed, verified material: call notes, a transcript, real metrics.
- A short, repeatable case-study structure.

## How it fits (what we did vs didn't)
- We have the **method** (a customer-journalism / operator-portrait approach) and the **measurement**
  to source real numbers, but a generic, reusable capture template wasn't yet separated from the
  company-specific version. This is that generic template.

## Case-study structure
1. **The situation** - who they are, the problem, in their words.
2. **What we did** - the play(s), framed by the loop (signal → … → outcome). No mystique.
3. **The result** - verified numbers from the scorecard (replied/meetings/pipeline/cost-per-meeting).
4. **The quote** - a real line from the client, used with permission.
5. **The takeaway** - what transfers to the next prospect.

## Blueprint: stand it up at a new company
1. Decide the metric you'll capture **before** the engagement starts (so you measure from day one).
2. Keep witnessed material as you go - notes, transcripts, the scorecard.
3. Build the narrative only from **verified** material; never inflate a number or invent a quote.
4. Get explicit permission for names, logos, and quotes before publishing.
5. Bank it as reusable proof (Blueprint 19 step 6).

## Gotchas / hard rules
- **Verified only** - every number traces to the scorecard; every quote is real and permissioned.
- **Permission before publication** - names, logos, quotes.
- **Measure from day one** - you can't reconstruct a baseline after the fact.

## Cost
Free. The asset compounds: each verified case study makes the next sale easier.
